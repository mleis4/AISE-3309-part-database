# AISE 3309 Assignment 3: Mock Rubric (unofficial)

> **Unofficial.** This rubric uses the six grading criteria from the Assignment 3 handout and the level structure of the Assignment 4 rubric. Its level descriptions come from the A3 task requirements. The AI criterion is based on the A3 AI policy (`explanation.md`, TA questioning) and the A4 "AI Process and Reflection" row. Weights copy A4's 1 pt per row and are not the official A3 weights.

| Criterion | Level 0: Non-Functional | Level 1: Partially Working | Level 2: Functional | Level 3: Robust | Level 4: Exemplary | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Schema Implementation (Task 1)** | CREATE TABLE statements are missing or do not run, or the tables do not match the refined Assignment 2 schema. | Some tables are created, but primary or foreign keys are missing or wrong, and the editor responses or DESCRIBE output are incomplete. | All relations are created with correct primary and foreign keys, and the editor response or a DESCRIBE query is shown for each table. | All keys, alternate keys (UNIQUE), NOT NULL and referential actions (ON DELETE / ON UPDATE) match the refined schema, and CHECK constraints enforce the domain rules. | The schema also uses well-justified indexes and triggers for constraints that keys and CHECKs cannot express. Data types are chosen deliberately, and every statement follows ISO SQL, with any MySQL-specific syntax noted. | 1 pt |
| **Data Insertion and Loading (Tasks 2 and 3)** | No data is loaded, or keys are duplicated and foreign keys do not join. | Fewer than three distinct INSERT types are shown, or the loaded data is below the required size, or some foreign key values do not match existing rows. | Three distinct INSERT types (for example, INSERT ... SELECT) are shown with their SELECT * results, and at least two relations hold a few thousand tuples and one holds hundreds. | All loaded data has unique keys and joins correctly across every relation. The generator source code is included, along with about five meaningful sample records per relation and a Workbench dump file. | The fabricated data is realistic enough to exercise the application: it respects every schema constraint and covers edge cases such as empty or partial results. The generator is documented and can be rerun. | 1 pt |
| **SELECT Queries (Task 4)** | Fewer than seven queries are submitted, or most fail to run. | Seven queries are submitted, but several are incorrect or most are simple single-table selections. | Seven queries run correctly, and all but one show an interesting SQL feature (multiple relations, subqueries, EXISTS, GROUP BY). | Queries are correct, diverse (each uses a different feature) and suited to the application, and the responses are shown without results thousands of lines long. | Queries are sophisticated, for example multi-table joins with aggregation, outer joins or nested subqueries with EXISTS. Each answers a realistic application question and is efficient on the loaded data. | 1 pt |
| **Data Modification Commands (Task 5)** | Fewer than three modification commands are submitted, or they fail to run. | Three commands are submitted, but they are simple single-tuple changes or of a single type. | Three commands run correctly, and each involves a complex feature (inserting a query result, updating several tuples, deleting a specific set). | INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE are each represented, and each command's effect on the database is shown and correct. | Commands use advanced features such as correlated subqueries and interact with the schema constraints (cascades, triggers or CHECK violations), and the report explains the effect. | 1 pt |
| **Views and Updatability (Task 6)** | No views are created, or they do not run. | Fewer than two views are created, or the query on each view or the modification attempt is missing. | Two views are created, a query on each is shown, and a modification attempt is shown for each. | The updatability of each view is explained correctly and agrees with what the system did in the modification script. | The views serve clear application purposes and contrast updatable and non-updatable cases, and the explanation cites the specific rules involved (joins, aggregates, DISTINCT, WITH CHECK OPTION). | 1 pt |
| **Report and Code Archive** | The PDF report or the .zip archive is missing. | The report is missing statements or screenshots for several tasks, or the archive does not follow the required folder structure. | The report includes the SQL, screenshots and sample data for Tasks 1 to 6, and the archive contains /SQL, /SRC and /DUMP with the required file names. | The report is well organized, with every statement next to its system response, and the archive matches the report exactly. | The report is polished and easy to navigate, with a table of contents, consistent formatting and concise explanations, and a grader can rebuild the database from the archive alone. | 1 pt |
| **AI Use and Understanding** | AI-written queries are submitted with no `explanation.md`, or the team cannot explain the submitted work. | `explanation.md` covers only some queries, or the explanations are superficial and show little verification of the AI output. | `explanation.md` explains the logic, syntax and execution of every query, modification and view in Tasks 4 to 6. | The explanations are accurate and specific to this schema, and every member can answer TA questions about any statement. | The team also documents its AI prompts, what the AI got right and wrong, and how output was checked against the loaded data, which feeds directly into the A4 reflection. | 1 pt |
