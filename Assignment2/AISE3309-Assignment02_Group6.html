1.3  Enhanced ER features, constraints and assumptions

  • Specialization. SharedProject is a subclass of BOM. Participation is {Optional} because most BOMs stay private. There is one subclass, so a disjoint/overlapping constraint does not apply. The subclass inherits the BOM's owner and parts, so the customer who shared a project and the parts it uses are not recorded twice.
  • Composition. BOM and ShoppingCart are parts of a Customer, and Listing is part of a Distributor. They cannot exist without the owner and are deleted with it (section 2.4).
  • Composite attributes. Customer.Name, Customer.BillingAddress and Part.Dimensions.
  • Derived attributes. /AvgStarRating, /NumberOfParts, /UnpricedParts, /TotalCost, /Subtotal, /ShippingPrice and /TotalPrice (defined in Task 2).

Constraints that the diagram cannot show:

#Constraint
C1StarRating is an integer from 1 to 5. Every Quantity is greater than 0. UnitPrice, StockQuantity and ShippingFee are not negative.
C2Project simplification: a distributor has at most one listing for a given part. Assignment 1 describes a listing as "one distributor's offer for one part"; we go one step further and exclude packaging and price-break variants of the same part (reel, cut tape, bulk), storing only the distributor's standard offer.
C3A BOM selects only listings of parts it contains, and at most one listing for each of those parts.
C4AlternativeTo is symmetric, and a part is not an alternative to itself.
C5A SharedProject's BOM contains at least one part at all times.
C6A customer reviews a part at most once. Purchases are completed on the distributor's own website, so the system does not record them and cannot check that the reviewer bought the part.
C7The part offered by a listing never changes. An offer for a different part is a new listing.

Assumptions about stored values:

  • All money values (UnitPrice, ShippingFee and the derived totals) are in Canadian dollars and exclude tax.
  • Length, Width and Height are in millimetres. EstShipTime is in days.
  • Customer.Password holds a salted hash of the password, never the password itself.
  • ShoppingCart.Date_created is the date the cart was created, which is when its first item was added.

C3, C5 and C7 cannot be written as keys or CHECKs, so each operation that could break them is handled as follows:

C3INSERT or UPDATE on BOMSelectionRejected unless the listing's part has a BOMLine in the same BOM and the BOM has no other selection for that part.

3.  Task 3: Schema Evaluation

3.3.1  Schema flaws and 3NF

Flaws, redundancy and anomalies

We examined the schema for repeated facts and for insert, update and delete anomalies.

IssueAssessmentDecision
Customer addressIn Canada a PostCode usually implies City and Province, which would be a transitive dependency. It is not a dependency in this system: customers can be in any country, postal code formats are not unique across countries, and one code can cover more than one municipality.Not treated as an FD. The address stays in Customer and is always read as a whole.
Derived valuesStoring /TotalCost, /Subtotal or /AvgStarRating would create a second copy that goes out of date whenever a price, quantity or review changes (update anomaly).None is stored. They are computed by queries or views.
Natural keys used as FKsEmail, distributor Name and the part key appear in several relations. Changing one must change every reference.Accepted, because surrogate IDs are not allowed. The FKs use ON UPDATE CASCADE (2.4).
PartAlternativeA symmetric pair could be stored twice as (A, B) and (B, A), which is redundant and can become inconsistent.Each pair is stored once (C4).
BOMLine and BOMSelectionThe link between a selection and its line is not a foreign key. It goes through the part of the listing, so C3 needs triggers on several operations.Accepted and specified in 2.4. Sections 3.3.2 and 3.3.3 explain why the two relations are not merged.
Shared project partsA separate part list per shared project would repeat the BOM's lines.Avoided by the specialization. A shared project uses its BOM's BOMLine rows.
Insert and delete anomaliesA part can exist with no listing, a distributor with no listings, and a customer with no BOM or cart. Deleting the last listing of a part does not delete the part.No anomaly. Each fact is stored in one relation.

Normalization of every relation

Each diagram shows the relation's attributes (primary key shaded and underlined) and its functional dependencies. A dot marks a determinant attribute and an arrow points to each dependent attribute. The tests used are:

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: no non-prime attribute depends on a proper subset of any candidate key.
  • 3NF: for every non-trivial FD X → A, X is a superkey or A is a prime attribute.

A prime attribute is one that belongs to some candidate key. The tests are applied to every candidate key, not only the primary key.

1. Customer

FD1: Email → Username, Password, FirstName, LastName, HouseAddress, UnitNum, City, Province, Country, PostCode
FD2: Username → Email, Password, FirstName, LastName, HouseAddress, UnitNum, City, Province, Country, PostCode
Candidate keys: {Email}; {Username}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: Both candidate keys (Email and Username) are single attributes, so no partial dependency is possible.
  • 3NF: The only determinants are Email (PK) and Username (AK), and both are superkeys. No non-key attribute determines another (see the customer address issue above).

2. Part

FD1: (ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum) → Name, Description, Category, Length, Width, Height, Datasheet_link
Candidate keys: {ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: The non-key attributes describe one specific part. A manufacturer makes many parts, and another manufacturer can use the same part number, so neither key attribute alone determines them.
  • 3NF: Category does not determine Name, the dimensions or the datasheet, and two parts can share a Name. The only determinant is the key.

3. Distributor

FD1: Name → Website, ShippingFee
FD2: Website → Name, ShippingFee
Candidate keys: {Name}; {Website}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: Both candidate keys (Name and Website) are single attributes, so no partial dependency is possible.
  • 3NF: Website determines Name and ShippingFee, but Website is a candidate key (no two distributors share a website), so its determinant is a superkey.

4. Listing

FD1: (DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum) → ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum, UnitPrice, StockQuantity, EstShipTime, LastUpdated
FD2: (DistributorName, ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum) → Distributor_PartNum, UnitPrice, StockQuantity, EstShipTime, LastUpdated
Candidate keys: {DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum}; {DistributorName, ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: Checked against both candidate keys. A distributor part number (SKU) is unique only within its distributor, so price, stock, shipping time and the part offered need the whole primary key. For the alternate key, a distributor alone or a part alone does not fix a price or a stock level. No attribute depends on a proper subset of either key.
  • 3NF: UnitPrice, StockQuantity, EstShipTime and LastUpdated are independent of each other. Both determinants are candidate keys, so both are superkeys.

5. ShoppingCart

FD1: CustomerEmail → Date_created
Candidate keys: {CustomerEmail}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: Single-attribute key, so no partial dependency is possible.
  • 3NF: One non-key attribute, so no transitive dependency is possible.

6. BOM

FD1: (CustomerEmail, BOM_name) → Date_created
Candidate keys: {CustomerEmail, BOM_name}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: Date_created belongs to one named BOM of one customer. A customer has many BOMs and a name can be reused by other customers, so neither key attribute alone determines it.
  • 3NF: One non-key attribute, so no transitive dependency is possible.

7. SharedProject

FD1: (CustomerEmail, BOM_name) → Title, Description, Date_shared
FD2: Title → CustomerEmail, BOM_name, Description, Date_shared
Candidate keys: {CustomerEmail, BOM_name}; {Title}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: Title, Description and Date_shared describe one shared BOM, which is identified only by the whole primary key. The alternate key Title is a single attribute.
  • 3NF: Title determines the other attributes, but Title is a candidate key, so it is a superkey. Description and Date_shared determine nothing.

8. PartAlternative

No non-trivial FDs.
Candidate keys: {Part1_ManufacturerName, Part1_PartNum, Part2_ManufacturerName, Part2_PartNum}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: All-key relation: there are no non-key attributes.
  • 3NF: All-key relation: there are no non-key attributes.

9. BOMLine

FD1: (CustomerEmail, BOM_name, ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum) → Quantity
Candidate keys: {CustomerEmail, BOM_name, ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: Quantity is how many of one part one BOM needs. It changes with the BOM and with the part, so it depends on the whole key.
  • 3NF: One non-key attribute, so no transitive dependency is possible.

10. BOMSelection

No non-trivial FDs.
Candidate keys: {CustomerEmail, BOM_name, DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: All-key relation: there are no non-key attributes.
  • 3NF: All-key relation: there are no non-key attributes.

11. CartLine

FD1: (CustomerEmail, DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum) → Quantity
Candidate keys: {CustomerEmail, DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: Quantity is how many units of one listing one cart holds, so it depends on the whole key.
  • 3NF: One non-key attribute, so no transitive dependency is possible.

12. Review

FD1: (CustomerEmail, ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum) → ReviewText, StarRating
Candidate keys: {CustomerEmail, ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum}

  • 1NF: every attribute holds a single atomic value.
  • 2NF: A customer reviews many parts and a part has many reviewers, so the text and the rating depend on the whole key.
  • 3NF: ReviewText does not determine StarRating, or the reverse.

Result: all 12 relations are in 1NF, 2NF and 3NF. There is no violation to resolve, so no relation is decomposed here.

3.3.2  Opportunities for combining relations

Two relations can be combined without repeating rows when they describe the same things, which happens for 1:1 relationships, superclass/subclass pairs and relationship relations over the same entities. We found three candidates.

CandidateCombined without redundancy?Decision and query justification
Customer + ShoppingCart (1:1, same key)Yes. Date_created would become a nullable column of Customer and no row is repeated.Rejected. Sign-in reads Customer on every visit and never needs the cart. Cart queries join CartLine to Listing and do not need the customer's credentials or address. A customer with no cart would store a NULL, and CartLine's FK would have to point at Customer, which hides the rule that lines belong to a cart.
BOM + SharedProject (superclass/subclass, same key)Yes. Title, Description and Date_shared would become nullable columns of BOM.Rejected. The most common query on shared projects is browsing or searching all of them by title, and it reads only the small SharedProject relation. Opening a private BOM never needs the sharing columns. Most BOMs are private, so the merged relation would be mostly NULLs, and Title could not be a simple NOT NULL alternate key.
BOMLine + BOMSelection (a selection belongs to one line of the BOM)No. No row is repeated, since the merged relation is BOMLine with nullable DistributorName and Distributor_PartNum. But the part of the selected listing would then be stored both in the line and in Listing.Rejected. Merging would remove one join from the BOM cost query (BOMSelection), although Listing is still needed for UnitPrice. It would also let the key enforce "one listing per line". We still keep them separate: the merged relation is not in BCNF (3.3.3), the supplier choice is optional so two columns of a composite FK would be NULL on many lines, and the frequent BOM queries (open a BOM, upload a CSV, copy a shared project) read parts and quantities only.

No other pair can be combined. Every remaining pair is linked by a 1:* or *:* relationship, so merging would repeat the attributes of one side: Distributor with Listing would repeat Website and ShippingFee on every listing, Part with Listing would repeat the part description for every distributor, and BOM with BOMLine would repeat Date_created on every line.

The design that would not be in BCNF

The merge considered in 3.3.2 gives BOMLine (CustomerEmail, BOM_name, ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum, Quantity, DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum):

Figure 3. FDs of the rejected merged BOMLine. FD2 has a determinant that is not a superkey.
  • FD1: (CustomerEmail, BOM_name, ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum) → Quantity, DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum. The determinant is the key.
  • FD2: (DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum) → ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum. A listing offers exactly one part. This determinant is not a superkey, so the relation is not in BCNF.
  • It is still in 3NF, because the attributes on the right of FD2 belong to the key (they are prime).

Its BCNF decomposition. Decomposing on FD2 gives:

  • R1 (DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum, ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum)
  • R2 (CustomerEmail, BOM_name, DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum, Quantity)

This decomposition is lossless, because the common attributes (DistributorName, Distributor_PartNum) are the key of R1. R1 is a projection of Listing, so it would not need to be stored again. R2 does not fit the system, for two reasons. A line could exist only after a listing is chosen, but an uploaded BOM has parts and quantities with no supplier yet. And FD1 is not preserved: Quantity would be keyed by the listing, and nothing would stop a BOM from holding two listings of the same part.

What the schema does instead. The schema never forms the merged relation. It maps the two relationships of the ER diagram separately: BOMLine for Contains (part and quantity) and BOMSelection for Selects (chosen listing). Both are in BCNF. These two relations are not by themselves a decomposition of the merged relation. They share only (CustomerEmail, BOM_name), so joining just the two would pair every part of a BOM with every selected listing of that BOM: a BOM with two parts and two selections would give four rows, two of them wrong. The pairing is recovered through Listing, which records the part of each listing:

BOMLine ⟕ (BOMSelection ⋈ Listing)

The inner join attaches its part to each selection. The left outer join is on (CustomerEmail, BOM_name, ManufacturerName, Manufacturer_PartNum), and it keeps the lines that have no selection. By C3 every selection matches exactly one line and a line matches at most one selection, so this expression returns exactly the rows of the merged relation, with nothing lost and no spurious row.

Merged BOMLine (3NF only)BOMLine + BOMSelection (BCNF, chosen)

Decision: keep the BCNF design. The most frequent BOM operations (open a BOM, upload a BOM from CSV, copy a shared project) work on parts and quantities only and never touch the supplier choice. The price we accept is one extra join in the cost query, on indexed key columns, and the triggers listed in 2.4. In return no fact is stored twice and no column is NULL.
